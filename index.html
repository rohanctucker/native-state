      About it

      Every living thing runs on proteins.

      They’re tiny machines, each folded from one long chain. Their shape decides what they do. In 2020 an AI called AlphaFold learned to predict those shapes, and its database now holds over 200 million of them.

      Pick one up.

      See it in your room

      Tap the button on the model to place it in your room. It will be about 30 cm across, roughly 60 million times bigger than the real thing.

      How it works

      Every protein here is a real prediction from the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database, made by Google DeepMind and EMBL-EBI. AlphaFold’s creators shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

      Holding one

      • Drag, or pinch with one hand on camera, to turn it. Each time you grab it, a new fact pops up.
      • Spread two fingers, or pinch with both hands and pull apart, to unfold it into its sequence. Let go and it folds back.
      • Tap any point to see which amino acid it is.

      The three looks

      Ribbon is how scientists usually draw proteins: spirals are helices, flat arrows are strands. Here they’re estimated from the shape of the backbone. Atoms shows every atom except hydrogen at its real size, like the space-filling models chemists build. Glow traces the backbone as a line of light.

      The colors

      In Ribbon and Glow, color is AlphaFold’s confidence for each amino acid, from 0 to 100. Blue parts are probably right. Yellow and orange parts are guesses, and often have no fixed shape in real life, so here they drift.

      Unfolding

      A protein is a chain of amino acids, written as letters. Pull one apart and you see that chain. In the early 1960s Christian Anfinsen showed that the sequence alone holds the shape, and won the 1972 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. The animation here is a simple blend between the two shapes, not a folding simulation.

      Where the facts come from

      Facts come straight from UniProt, where scientists curate what is known about each protein, from DrugBank through UniProt, and from Wikipedia. Sizes and amino acid counts are measured from the structure itself. The short stories for the featured proteins were written for this site.
